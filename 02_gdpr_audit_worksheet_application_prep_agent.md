# GDPR audit worksheet: Application Prep Agent (Phases 1–5)

*Based on [`01_data_processing_brief_application_prep_agent.md`](01_data_processing_brief_application_prep_agent.md). Two deployment set-ups are assessed, because the roles differ:*
- ***Demo:** job seekers use my public form directly. This is today's situation.*
- ***Coach:** a bootcamp or university career-services team runs it for its candidates. This is the go-to-market sprint 1–2 plan.*

*First-pass assessment, not legal advice.*

## CFU 1: Recognize

- **Personal data present:**
  - contact data (email, name, phone, address);
  - employment and education history;
  - free-text achievements;
  - inferred suitability data (fit score, strong/partial/gap labels);
  - visitor IP addresses;
  - incidental third-party names (recruiters, references).
- **Special-category data (Art. 9):** not requested, but **can be present** if candidates include it: a photo (which can reveal ethnicity or religion), disability status, a health-related gap, religious employers, union roles. Outputs could also *mention* it, for example if the model writes about a career gap. Age, nationality and gender aren't Art. 9 data, but they are discrimination-relevant (German anti-discrimination law, AGG) and can be inferred from dates, names and language skills.
- **Crosses an EU border?** **Yes.** CV text goes to OpenAI's US processing by default. The report goes to Google (Gmail). Visitor IPs go to Google Fonts. Where the n8n server sits is unknown.

**Purpose drift flags:**

1. **Demo set-up:** none inside the workflow. The CV is used only to make the requested report, and nothing is reused for training, RAG or analytics (checked: the Pinecone query is a fixed string).
2. **Execution history and the Gmail Sent folder** keep CV-derived data beyond the purpose it was collected for. Nobody decided to keep it; it's a side effect of the tools' default settings. That breaks storage limitation (Art. 5(1)(e)).
3. **Coach set-up:** a CV the career service collected at enrolment, or for general career support, is **sent to a new US AI processor for automated profiling**. The purpose is broadly the same (career support), but the means are new and weren't in the original privacy notice. That needs a compatibility check (Art. 6(4)) and an updated notice.
4. **Sprint 3 "saved candidate profiles"** would be a new purpose (storing and reusing profiles). It needs its own basis and retention.

## Phase 3: Lawful basis assessment

| Purpose | Proposed lawful basis | One-line justification | Legal review? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** Generate the pack (demo) | **Contract, Art. 6(1)(b)** | The user asks for a service; processing their CV is objectively necessary to deliver it. A free service is still a contract. | No |
| **P1** Generate the pack (coach) | **Legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f)**, of the career service, or **contract** if career support is part of the candidate's enrolment terms | Depends on what the client's enrolment contract and privacy notice already cover | **Yes, TBD.** The client's counsel must pick the basis |
| **P2** Fit-score gate | **Contract, Art. 6(1)(b)** (demo) / same basis as P1 (coach) | The score is part of the requested analysis; the gate decides the format of the reply | No (but see Art. 22 in Phase 4) |
| **P3** Fact-check | **Contract, Art. 6(1)(b)** | A quality step within the same service; no new data, no new recipient | No |
| **P4** Email delivery | **Contract, Art. 6(1)(b)** | Delivery is how the service is performed | No |
| **P5** Execution history for operations and debugging | **Legitimate interests, Art. 6(1)(f)** (LIA below) | Only defensible with short retention and errors-only logging | **Yes, TBD** on the retention period |
| **P6** Copy of reports in the Gmail Sent folder | **None** | There's no purpose for it. It's a side effect of the mail set-up | Stop: change the delivery method |
| **P7** Google Fonts loading (IP to Google) | **None defensible** | Self-hosted fonts do the same job without the transfer (a German court held dynamic Google Fonts unlawful: LG München I, 3 O 17493/20, Jan 2022) | Stop: self-host |
| **Incidental Art. 9 data** in CVs | Art. 6 as for P1; **Art. 9 condition: TBD** | No Art. 9(2) condition clearly fits data a candidate volunteers in a CV. The safer route is minimisation (see Phase 4) | **Yes, TBD** |
| **Future: sprint 3 saved profiles** | **Consent, Art. 6(1)(a)**, or contract | Storing profiles for reuse is a new, optional purpose | **Yes, TBD** before building |

**Legitimate Interests Assessment: P5, execution history**

1. **Legitimate interest?** Yes. Diagnosing failed runs (an unreadable PDF, an API timeout) and proving what the system did is a real operational need.
2. **Necessary?** Only partly. Keeping *every successful* run, with the full CV, is not necessary. Keeping *failed* runs for a short period is. Less intrusive option: in the workflow settings, set "save successful executions" to none and keep error executions for 7 days.
3. **Balancing?** With error-only, 7-day retention, the impact is low and users would reasonably expect it. With indefinite retention of every CV, the user's interests override. **Verdict:** passes only after the settings change. The retention period is TBD (legal review).

**LIA: P1 in the coach set-up.** The interest (better career support) is legitimate. Necessity is plausible. Balancing depends on candidates being told their CV goes to a US AI provider, and being able to opt out. I can't complete it without the client's privacy notice, so it is **TBD – legal review**.

## Phase 4: Risk and rights analysis

**Special-category data (Art. 9).**
- The system doesn't ask for special-category data, but CVs often contain it (photo, disability status, health-related gaps, religious or union roles), and the model can repeat it in the report.
- No Art. 9(2) condition clearly covers this. "Manifestly made public" (9(2)(e)) doesn't apply to a CV sent privately, and explicit consent isn't currently collected.
- The practical answer is minimisation:
  - tell users to remove photos and sensitive details before uploading;
  - instruct every LLM prompt to ignore and never repeat them;
  - add a consent checkbox if a lawyer says one is needed.
- **TBD – legal review.**

**Automated decision-making (Art. 22).**
- The fit-score gate is a **solely automated decision**: below 50, the user gets a short note instead of the full pack, with no human review.
- I don't consider it to have legal or similarly significant effects. It only changes the format of a free advisory reply, the candidate can still apply for the job, and nothing goes to an employer.
- The risk rises in the coach set-up if the score drives who gets coaching support. Reading the score as "decisive" for someone else's decision about a person is the CJEU *SCHUFA* (C-634/21) logic.
- **Safeguards to add anyway:**
  - a "send me the full pack anyway" option;
  - an explanation of how the score is calculated (100 minus 10 per partial, minus 20 per gap);
  - a contact address to contest it;
  - in the coach version, a rule that the score is never used to select candidates.

**DPIA trigger (EDPB criteria).**

| Criterion | Applies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Evaluation or scoring | ✅ | Fit score of people against job requirements |
| 2. Automated decision with significant effect | ❌ (⚠️ coach) | Gate affects only the reply format |
| 3. Systematic monitoring | ❌ | One-off submissions |
| 4. Sensitive data | ⚠️ | Incidental Art. 9 data in CVs |
| 5. Large scale | ❌ now / ⚠️ sprint 3 | Demo volumes; 200+ reports a month is still modest |
| 6. Matching or combining datasets | ❌ | Research is about companies, not people |
| 7. Vulnerable data subjects | ⚠️ coach | Job seekers who depend on their bootcamp or outplacement provider (power imbalance) |
| 8. Innovative technology | ✅ | LLM-based profiling and generation |
| 9. Prevents exercising rights | ❌ | — |

Two criteria clearly apply (1, 8) and up to three more apply in the coach set-up, so a **DPIA is required before the client pilot**. For the demo it is borderline, and I'd do a short one anyway.

**Data subject rights friction.**

| Right | Main problem | Can the current design respond within one month? |
|---|---|---|
| **Access (Art. 15)** | No user record or lookup. Data sits in n8n executions and the Gmail Sent folder, and is findable only by manual search on the email address. OpenAI-side copies can't be retrieved | **Partly.** Manual and slow; no contact address is published for requests |
| **Erasure (Art. 17)** | Each n8n execution and Sent email must be deleted by hand. OpenAI's up-to-30-day abuse-monitoring copy can't be deleted on request | **Partly.** Possible, but not documented. Fixed by not storing in the first place |
| **Objection / contesting profiling (Art. 21, 22(3))** | Nothing in the design lets a user dispute a score; a rerun gives a new score but no explanation | **No.** Needs a contact route and a written explanation of the score logic |

## Phase 5: Law stacking check

- **AI Act:**
  - **Limited risk (Art. 50)**, from my previous lab. It is **high-risk (Annex III 4(a))** only if a deployer uses the score to select candidates.
  - The AI Act adds what GDPR doesn't: machine-readable marking of AI-generated text (Art. 50(2)), AI literacy for coaches (Art. 4), and a hard classification line on use for selection that GDPR doesn't draw.
- **ePrivacy:**
  - No tracking cookies or pixels are known in my code (the n8n form's own cookies are unverified).
  - Google Fonts isn't a cookie issue but an IP transfer (a GDPR issue), so self-host the fonts.
  - Report emails are transactional, so ePrivacy marketing-consent rules don't apply. **Currently satisfied**, subject to checking the n8n form.
- **Data Act:** **N/A.** No connected products, IoT data or cloud-switching scenario.
