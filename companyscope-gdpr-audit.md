**Three-part LIA.** (1) **Interest:** reliably distinguish counterparties, prepare a limited company briefing and diagnose incidents. None of these purposes requires expanding into person screening. (2) **Necessity:** prefer an exact register-ID lookup, registry-only or manual research, company name plus city for search, redacted/local synthesis and event metadata for support. Record outcome and cost/accuracy tradeoffs per purpose. Where an equally effective less intrusive approach works, use it. (3) **Balancing:** professional details in context may be expected, while onward LLM disclosure, a home address, stale adverse claims and persistent copies may not be. Restrict purpose and recipients, suppress unnecessary details, provide notice and objection paths, check sources and expiry, and stop if individual interests override. This is not a signed LIA or a conclusion that all five purposes pass.

## 4. Risk and rights

**Art. 9 and Art. 10.** Public snippets or misplaced free text can contain or support inferences about health, politics, religion, trade union membership or other protected traits, despite the company's research purpose. No Art. 9 condition is established, and information being searchable is not proof that the person manifestly made it public under Art. 9(2)(e). Criminal-offence references also need a separate Art. 10 assessment. Exclude this material from external calls and outputs, quarantine or delete unexpected occurrences under a controlled procedure, and prohibit person-focused inference.

**Art. 22.** The prototype does not itself make a solely automated decision that has legal or similarly significant effects on a person, so no Art. 22 exception is relied on. The output is a briefing, not a person ranking or a counterparty rejection, but a downstream workflow could make it effectively decisive. Require documented source verification and authority to override before any consequential use, and reassess Art. 22, applicable exceptions and safeguards if a future integration gates access to contracts or services for people.

**DPIA.** The nine-criterion screen supports several risk signals even without automated decisions about people. Treat the current unredacted public-web plus LLM workflow as likely to require a DPIA before live client deployment due to dataset combination, new technology and credible sensitive-data exposure, with final assessment by the controller after checking national Art. 35(4) lists. Re-screen a demonstrably narrower version rather than assuming that two checkboxes alone decide the issue. Consult the authority before processing if high residual risk cannot be mitigated under Art. 36.

| EDPB criterion | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| Evaluation or scoring | Not intended for people. Report observations could still indirectly characterize identifiable people. |
| Automated decision with significant effects | Not present in the MVP. No outcome action shown. |
| Systematic monitoring | Not shown. On-demand queries, no recurring individual tracking. |
| Sensitive or highly personal data | Credible incidental risk. Open web can include sensitive or criminal material and exported logs can retain raw results. Scale is a separate criterion. |
| Large scale | Not demonstrated. Classroom prototype and unknown deployment volumes. |
| Matching or combining datasets | Yes where the same individual is identifiable across registry and public sources of different contexts. The code joins by company, not a person key. |

| Preventing a right, service or contract | Not shown. A cross-border transfer is not itself this criterion, though it warrants a separate assessment and may make rights harder to exercise. |

**Rights friction.** Access and rectification are not demonstrably timely because an individual can appear in raw executions, source excerpts, Markdown and exported copies without a cross-store lookup or correction process. Erasure or restriction is not demonstrably timely because no inventory-wide deletion workflow or vendor assistance has been tested, and exceptions and backups need handling. Art. 21 objection to legitimate-interest processing has no documented intake or suppression route, and there is no documented subject-facing Art. 14 plan for indirect sources. Assign an owner, map access/correction/deletion by execution, person and source, test a request and meet Art. 12's one-month deadline unless a lawful extension applies.
