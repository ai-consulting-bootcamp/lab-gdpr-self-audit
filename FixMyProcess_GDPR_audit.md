**Repurposing flags**

1. **Customer and colleague data (highest risk).** Kleinanzeigen collected this data for marketplace or employment purposes. Pasting it into the tool sends it to a US LLM and stores it in Notion and Slack: a new purpose with no compatibility check (Art. 6(4)).
2. **Problem text reused for the pipeline.** The employee writes the text to get their own fix report; it is then reused for the enablement team (3) and the Board (4). This is compatible if employees are told in advance. Today there is no privacy notice.
3. **Planned department summaries.** Monthly summaries by department would reuse problem text, flags and feedback to compare teams. That moves the purpose from improving processes to evaluating staff and needs its own assessment before it is built.

**Legitimate Interests Assessment, purposes (1) to (5) and (7) to (9)**

1. **Is the interest legitimate?** Yes. Kleinanzeigen needs to cut manual work and keep AI use within its internal AI policy; without a sanctioned route, employees tend to use unapproved tools.
2. **Is the processing necessary?** Partly. The fix needs the problem and the department, not names. Less intrusive options exist and should be used: strip names and customer details before the LLM call, send only the summary to Slack and Notion, and set a retention period.
3. **Does the individual's interest override?** No for the submitting employee, if the options above are applied, a privacy notice is given and the data is never used to judge people: employees expect work input to be processed. Yes for named colleagues and customers, who have no such expectation, which is why their data must be kept out.

Purpose (6) and the planned department summaries: **TBD: legal review**, because the balancing test depends on how the summaries would be used.

## Phase 4: Risk and rights analysis

**Special category data (Art. 9).** Not asked for, but free text can reveal it, for example a colleague's sick leave (health) or a works council matter (trade union membership), and the reports can repeat it. It is not inferable from the flags or ratings. No Art. 9(2) condition fits this purpose, so the design must filter it before the LLM call and delete it when found.

**Automated decision-making (Art. 22).** No. The outputs concern processes and tools, the employee decides what to do, and no output has a legal or similarly significant effect on a person; the red-case Board decision concerns the use case. This changes if flags, feedback or department summaries feed into performance judgments: then human review, a right to contest and an explanation of the logic are needed. A simple way to contest a red flag should be added now.

**DPIA trigger (EDPB criteria).** Two criteria clearly apply: employees are vulnerable data subjects because of the power imbalance, and an LLM agent with retrieval is innovative technology. Combining employee input with web, news and internal documents partly meets the matching criterion, and evaluation and systematic monitoring would apply once department summaries are built. A DPIA is therefore required before the pilot.

**Data subject rights friction.** Access is hard because there is no user ID: a case sits in Airtable, Notion, Slack, n8n history and vendor logs, linked only by the reference code, so a timely answer depends on the employee having kept that code. Erasure cannot be handled in time today, as there is no retention period, no deletion procedure across five stores, and vendor-side logs are outside direct control. Employees can object only by not using the tool, and named colleagues or customers cannot exercise any of these rights in practice.

## Phase 6: Compliance memo

**To:** Data Protection Officer, Kleinanzeigen
**From:** Ayhan Demir
**Re:** GDPR review of Fix My Process

**Bottom line: proceed with conditions.** The system can run a pilot under legitimate interests, but no real employee data should enter it until processor contracts, transfer safeguards and input controls are in place.

Fix My Process asks for no names, yet the free-text description will often contain colleagues, customers and the employee's own views. That text reaches up to eleven services, at least eight with US infrastructure, all on free plans with no DPAs, no EU regions and no retention period. Employees get no privacy notice. Outputs concern processes rather than people, so Article 22 is not triggered today.

**Top three actions**

1. **Contracts and transfers before any real data flows.** Sign DPAs with every processor (OpenAI, Cohere, Pinecone, Tavily, Airtable, Notion, Slack, Render and the n8n host), confirm DPF certification or SCCs for each US transfer, and use EU regions where offered. Replace GNews and the Ironhack n8n instance if their location and terms cannot be documented.
2. **DPIA before the pilot.** The system meets at least two EDPB criteria (employees as vulnerable data subjects, innovative technology). Include the LIA, a retention schedule (I propose 90 days for cases, 30 for error records) and an Article 13 privacy notice.
3. **Minimise at input and lock the purpose.** Filter names and customer details before the LLM call, send only summaries to Slack and Notion, and state that case data will not be used to evaluate teams or individuals. Department summaries need their own assessment before they are built.

**Residual risks**

- Filters will miss some personal and special-category data in free text.
- US transfers rest on the Data Privacy Framework, upheld by the General Court but now under appeal at the CJEU; a ruling against it would force SCCs or EU-only vendors.
- Department-level records make team comparison easy, whatever the policy says.

**What this memo is not.** It is not a legal opinion, a DPIA or a certification. It is a first consultant review based on the data processing brief. Please engage legal counsel, and involve the works council for employee data, before relying on it for compliance decisions.

## Stretch: Data protection by design checklist

**Highest-risk processing activity:** the free-text problem description, which can carry colleague and customer data and goes to OpenAI and then into Airtable, Notion and Slack (purposes 1 to 4).

| Design principle | Current state of your system | Pass / Fail / Unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Data minimisation | No name or login is collected, but the free text is not filtered; only an on-screen warning. Red cases send the full text to Slack | Fail |
| Purpose binding | Nothing technical restricts the data to purposes 1 to 4; the same records could feed department summaries | Fail |
| Access controls | Who can open the Airtable base, Notion pages, Slack channels and the Ironhack n8n instance is not documented | Unknown |
| Retention enforcement | No retention period; records stay until someone deletes them | Fail |
| Subject rights workflow | Only manual search and deletion across five stores, linked by reference code | Fail |
| Incident response | The error handler alerts on workflow failures, not on breaches; no 72-hour notification process | Fail |

**What needs to change**

- **Minimisation:** add a redaction step before the first LLM call that removes names, emails, phone numbers and customer IDs; send the Board the summary, not the full text.
- **Purpose binding:** keep reports and Board data in separate tables or views, and record in the DPIA that case data cannot be reused for evaluating teams without a new assessment.
- **Access controls:** move to company-owned workspaces, limit the Airtable base and Notion pages to the enablement team and Board, and document who has access.
- **Retention:** a scheduled n8n job that deletes or anonymises cases after 90 days and error records after 30, and shorter n8n execution history.
- **Subject rights:** one lookup by reference code that lists and deletes the record in Airtable, Notion and Slack, with a log of each request.
- **Incident response:** add Fix My Process to Kleinanzeigen's breach procedure, name an owner, and get breach notice terms into each vendor DPA so the 72-hour clock (Art. 33) can be met.
