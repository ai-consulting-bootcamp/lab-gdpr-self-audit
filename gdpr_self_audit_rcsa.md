## Phase 3 – Lawful basis (Art. 6)

**Chosen basis: Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest.** Art. 6(1)(c) (legal obligation) is tempting because banks must run proper risk management (§25a KWG, MaRisk, CRR), but those rules do not prescribe *this* processing of names in an AI tool. Consent (6(1)(a)) does not work for employees because of the power imbalance. In Germany, the employment context also requires a look at §26 BDSG (its scope is limited after CJEU C‑34/21), which needs legal confirmation.

**Legitimate interest assessment**
| Test | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Effective, evidence-based operational risk management and regulatory compliance (MaRisk, CRR operational risk). This is a legitimate and well-documented interest. |
| **Necessity** | Owner role and identity are needed for accountability. Loss event **facts** (what happened, amount, date, process) are needed for the challenge. **Names of involved employees and customer references are not needed**, so they must be removed or pseudonymised before processing. Owner name need not go to the LLM; the role is enough. |
| **Balancing** | Employees expect their RCSA answers to be reviewed as part of their job, so the impact is low **if** the data is used only for process risk. The risk rises if challenge flags are used to judge individuals, or if sensitive loss narratives go to a US vendor. **Safeguards:** pseudonymisation before LLM calls; results per process, not per person; no HR use (written purpose limitation); access only for the ORM team; retention limit; information notice to staff; works council agreement (BetrVG §87(1) Nr. 6). **With these safeguards, the balance favours the bank.** |

**Art. 9 / Art. 10 data:** no lawful condition is planned for health or criminal-offence data in this tool. The design must **filter or redact** it before it reaches the agent (rule-based plus NER redaction, and a manual flag on "internal fraud" events).

## Phase 4 – Risks and rights

**Special category data:** not intended, but **likely to appear incidentally** in loss event narratives (health: injuries, sickness absence; Art. 10: internal fraud). Mitigation: redaction layer, exclude "Internal fraud" event-type narratives from LLM input or use only the structured fields.

**DPIA needed? Yes, likely.** Several EDPB DPIA criteria are met: employees as data subjects (power imbalance), new technology (LLM), evaluation of answers linked to people, possible sensitive data, and transfer outside the EEA. Two or more criteria normally mean a DPIA is required (Art. 35, EDPB WP248).

**Automated decision-making (Art. 22):** **not triggered.** There is no decision with legal or similarly significant effect on a person, and ratings concern processes and require human approval. This stays true only if challenge results are never used for HR decisions.

**Data subject rights**
| Right | How the system supports it | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Information (Art. 13/14) | None yet | Staff privacy notice for RCSA; Art. 14 notice covers employees named in loss events |
| Access (Art. 15) | Files are searchable | Need a way to export all data linked to an owner, incl. challenge flags |
| Rectification (Art. 16) | Owner can edit answers and reject challenges | OK; log changes |
| Erasure (Art. 17) | None | Retention rule; regulatory record-keeping can limit erasure (Art. 17(3)(b)) |
| Objection (Art. 21) | None | Required because the basis is legitimate interest; define a handling process |
| Not subject to ADM (Art. 22) | Human approval step | Enforce it (AI Act self-audit Gap 3) |

**Transfers:** OpenAI is US-based. Before use: verify EU–US Data Privacy Framework certification or SCCs plus a transfer impact assessment, the DPA, zero data retention or EU data residency options.

## Phase 5 – Law tracking

| Law | Applies? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **EU AI Act** | Yes, light | Limited risk / transparency (Art. 50(2)); Art. 4 AI literacy. Must stay out of Annex III 4(b) (see EU AI Act self-audit). |
| **DORA** | **Yes** | OpenAI and any hosting are ICT third-party services: register of information, risk assessment, contractual terms (Art. 28–30). |
| **MaRisk AT 9 / EBA outsourcing guidelines** | Yes | Possible outsourcing of a risk-management-related function; needs an outsourcing risk analysis. |
| **BDSG §26 / BetrVG §87(1) Nr. 6** | Yes (Germany) | Employee data processing; a technical system suited to monitoring performance requires works council co-determination. |
| **Banking secrecy / §25h KWG** | Yes | Customer references and fraud data. |
| **ePrivacy / TDDDG** | No (as designed) | Internal tool, no cookies or tracking on end-user devices. Revisit if it becomes a web SaaS with tracking. |
| **Data Act** | No | No connected products or IoT data. |
| **NIS2** | No (separately) | DORA is the sector-specific rule for banks. |

## Summary of actions
1. Pseudonymise and redact before LLM calls (names, customer refs, Art. 9/10 content).
2. DPIA before real data is used.
3. DPA, transfer assessment and retention settings with OpenAI; DORA register entry.
4. Staff privacy notice, objection process, retention schedule.
5. Purpose limitation: no HR use; works council agreement.
