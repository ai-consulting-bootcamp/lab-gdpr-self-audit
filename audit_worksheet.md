| Buyer context processing | Contract | Needed to run the report the user requested | No |
| Vendor research (public web data) | Legitimate interests | Reasonable to research a vendor's public compliance signal for risk purposes | Yes, LIA below not yet formalized |
| Report storage | Contract | Needed to deliver and retain the report the user requested | Yes, once a retention period is set |

**LIA for vendor research (legitimate interests):**

1. Is the interest legitimate? Yes. Buyers need public risk signal on a vendor before they sign a contract.
2. Is the processing necessary? Yes, but only for public and business-facing data. There is no need to intentionally collect data about identifiable private individuals.
3. Does the individual's interest override? Generally no, since the target of the research is a vendor company, not a person. Risk rises only if a named individual appears in a breach disclosure pulled by web search.

## Phase 4: Risk and rights analysis

**Special category data (Article 9):**
Not intentionally collected. It could be inferred if a public source names an individual's health or criminal status inside a vendor breach disclosure. No Article 9 condition currently applies because this is incidental, not designed. This needs a mitigation, such as filtering named individuals from report text.

**Automated decision-making (Article 22):**
The system does not make decisions about individuals. It scores vendors. The recommendation engine ranks alternative vendors, which is a business decision, not one with legal or similarly significant effect on a person. No Article 22 safeguard is currently required.

**DPIA trigger:**
Two criteria are close: large-scale data processing (if adopted broadly) and systematic monitoring (ongoing vendor watchlist checks). Two or more criteria applying generally means a DPIA is required. Recommend running one before wider rollout.

**Data subject rights friction:**
Right of access is the main concern. If a person's name appears in a stored report pulled from public web search, there is currently no process to locate and remove it on request. Right to erasure has the same gap. Right to object to profiling is low risk here, since no profiling of individuals happens.

## Phase 5: Law stacking check

**AI Act cross-check:** Prior review classified this system as limited risk / transparency. It does not fall under Annex III. GDPR adds the DPA, retention, and rights process obligations the AI Act does not cover on its own.

**ePrivacy check:** No cookies, tracking pixels, or device-level tracking currently in use. Not applicable at this stage.

**Data Act check:** No IoT or connected product data involved. Not applicable.
